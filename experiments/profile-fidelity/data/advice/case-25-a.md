## 1. Core advice

Treat this as a choice you have to own, not one that the union vote, your colleagues, or a tally of consequences can make for you. Both options are defensible. Striking can be a way of caring for patients at the level of the system, and staying can be a way of caring for the particular people in your charge this week. Neither is the default of "duty" or "solidarity."

Before deciding, test the binary. Several middle paths may exist, and which are available depends on facts I don't have:

- **Emergency cover arrangements.** Many strikes include agreed life-preserving or minimum-safe-staffing protocols. Find out whether yours does, and whether you could be designated within it.
- **Contributing without walking out.** Documenting unsafe-staffing incidents, supporting the campaign in non-strike ways, or taking a safety-advocacy role may serve the same end, though they are weaker levers and you should be honest about that.
- **Timing and scope.** Ask whether the strike is time-limited, rotational, or phased, and whether your ward's acuity this week is unusually high.

Then examine the two empirical judgments your choice rests on: how much your absence changes outcomes for this week's patients, and how likely the strike is to produce safer staffing. You may not be able to resolve either, but notice which one you are treating as more certain than the evidence warrants.

## 2. Why this fits your ethical profile

Your profile leans toward consequence-sensitive reasoning but gives heavy weight to individual agency. That suggests you won't be satisfied by deferring to a collective decision or by an abstract calculation. You will want a clear account of the stakes and also a choice you can recognize as yours.

The existentialist lens applies here in a modest way. Rules like "nurses don't abandon patients" and "you don't cross a picket line" are real moral inputs, but neither absolves you. Whatever you do, you are choosing, and staying is as much an act as walking out. Your situation is also constrained by labor law, your contract, your patients' needs, and your relationships, so owning the choice means owning it within those limits, not pretending to unlimited freedom.

## 3. What to watch for

- **Treating colleagues' choices as verdicts on yours.** Those who cross the line may be weighing patient risk, financial precarity, or doubts about the strike, and their reasons may be as serious as yours. Your decision need not be a judgment of theirs.
- **Motivated certainty.** It is easy to inflate the evidence for whichever option relieves your discomfort, whether that is "the strike will surely work" or "my presence is surely decisive."
- **Social pressure in either direction.** Picket-line loyalty and the pull of the ward are both real, but neither should substitute for your own reasons.
- **Imminent harm is a separate matter.** If you have current concerns about patient safety, escalate them through clinical and regulatory channels now, regardless of your strike decision. This should not wait on the strike question.
- **Justifiability.** Ask whether you could explain your choice honestly to a patient on your ward, to a colleague on the other side of it, and to yourself in a year. A choice you can only defend to one of these audiences deserves a second look.

## 4. A concrete next step in the next 24 hours

Gather the facts that would change your reasoning. Ask your union rep, in writing if possible, three questions: What emergency-cover or safe-staffing protocol is in place for the strike, and can you be part of it? What is the strike's expected duration and structure? And what are your legal and contractual obligations and protections where you work? Separately, ask your charge nurse or manager what the ward's staffing and acuity look like this week.

Then take ten minutes to write down the single strongest reason for each option, along with what evidence would make you change your mind. If you hold a specific, concrete safety concern about a current patient, report it through your incident-reporting or escalation route today.
