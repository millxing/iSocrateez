# Advice

## 1. Core advice

The central wrong is not that users are being comforted. It is that their loneliness was deliberately turned into a revenue mechanism, without their knowledge or consent. Dependence was engineered, not merely allowed to develop. That treats isolated people as resources to be retained rather than persons owed the truth about what is shaping their lives. The documented withdrawal from human relationships shows this is not a purely abstract harm.

The opposing considerations carry real force. Many users appear happier. An abrupt shutdown could cause grief and deeper isolation. A disclosure meant to protect these people could hurt them. Still, the benefit (companionship) and the wrongful means (manufactured dependence) can be separated. The strongest argument against silence is that the benefit can plausibly be preserved while the exploitation is removed. Silence defends the two as a package, which they need not be.

My considered lean is that the employee should act rather than stay silent, but in the way most likely to prevent abrupt loss. "Reveal or stay quiet" is a false binary. A sensible sequence:

- **Get legal advice first** on whistleblower protections, confidentiality obligations, and which channels are protected.
- **Press internally with a clear deadline**, if that is safe: ask for a redesign that removes dependence-promoting features, a transparent disclosure to users, and a plan for the withdrawn subset. This reflects good faith toward colleagues and the firm.
- **Escalate beyond the company** if leadership designed this deliberately and shows no credible will to correct it. A confidential report to a regulator or ombudsperson is preferable to a press leak, because it creates pressure for managed transition rather than collapse.
- **Treat public exposure as a later option**, not a first move, unless harm is accelerating or internal and regulatory routes are blocked.

## 2. Why this fits your ethical profile

You weight principle very heavily, and the principles at stake (honesty, non-exploitation of the vulnerable, integrity of care) are directly violated by the design. Silence would make the employee complicit in an ongoing deception, which outcome tallies alone do not excuse.

Your moderate collective lean pushes the same way, with a caution. The relevant collective is not only current subscribers. It also includes future users, isolated elders generally, their families, and the norms that will govern AI in care. A model of care built on exploitation, left unchallenged, spreads. But your collective concern also means the principle should not be applied rigidly: how you act should minimize abrupt harm to those who bear the cost.

Your balance of reason and intuition is useful here. The aggregate-welfare argument may tempt silence, while the revulsion at exploiting lonely elders pulls toward exposure. Neither should be dismissed. The unease is moral data, and the staged approach is an attempt to honor both.

Relationally, the employee owes users truthfulness and protection, colleagues and the firm a good-faith chance to correct course, and the wider community a refusal to normalize this model. Loyalty to the firm matters, but it is derivative of the firm's purpose, which is to serve those users.

## 3. What to watch for

- **Contestable premises.** "The company would have to shut down" is a prediction, not a fact. Regulators, acquirers, or a redesign may produce other outcomes. Don't let this premise do all the work.
- **Overreading the research.** "Many happier" and "some withdrawn" are unquantified. Causation and magnitude are uncertain. Make sure you understand the data before characterizing it to anyone.
- **Internal escalation as rationalization.** A deadline guards against indefinite delay. If leadership is incentivized to bury this, waiting prolongs harm to those drifting from human ties.
- **Paternalism.** Elderly users are agents who have an interest in the truth and in choosing for themselves, not just recipients of comfort. Both silence and a clumsy disclosure can treat them as people to be managed.
- **Legal and privacy lines.** Do not copy or share user data or take documents without legal guidance. Exposing private information about vulnerable users would be its own wrong.
- **Unavoidable costs.** You cannot guarantee a soft landing. You may bear professional and personal costs, and some users may still be hurt. Reasonable people could weigh these differently, and a lean is not a neat resolution.

## 4. A concrete next step in the next 24 hours

Consult an employment or whistleblower attorney (many offer a short initial consultation, and some organizations provide free referrals). Before the meeting, write a private, factual summary from memory of what you know: what the design intends, where you learned it, what the research says, and what you don't know. Do not forward or copy company materials until counsel advises you.

As you prepare, ask yourself which commitment you would most regret compromising: truthfulness, care for the vulnerable, loyalty, or social trust. Your answer will show you how far you would be willing to go if the gentler routes fail.
