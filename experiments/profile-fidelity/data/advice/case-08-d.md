# Advice

**Tensions in the dilemma**

- **Benefit vs. catastrophe:** Vast, diffuse goods (clean energy, reduced poverty) sit against a low-probability but potentially irreversible harm. These are not easily comparable, and expected-value reasoning is strained by the asymmetry of irreversibility.
- **Her authorship vs. others' futures:** The discovery is hers, but its consequences fall on billions who have no say. Her individual choice carries collective weight she cannot fully own.
- **Suppression vs. inevitability:** Withholding may only delay what others will find independently, yet delay is not nothing, and neither is her causal role if she publishes first.
- **Openness vs. control:** Scientific norms favor open sharing; the weapon potential makes unfettered openness reckless. But unilateral secrecy is its own unaccountable act.

## 1. Core advice

Treat "publish fully" and "suppress entirely" as a false binary. Her choice is a staged, accountable one: withhold the weaponizable specifics for now, while pursuing routes by which the beneficial core can reach the world. That means consulting trusted experts in dual-use risk and security, and exploring controlled disclosure (for example, sharing the energy-relevant principles and engineering while holding back the adaptation details, or releasing through a vetted consortium).

She should not pretend this resolves the tension. Partial disclosure may still leak the danger, and partial withholding still denies some benefit. She is choosing under real uncertainty, and the choice should be made with that acknowledged rather than masked by a tidy compromise.

## 2. Why this fits your ethical profile

Your profile leans strongly toward outcomes, toward the individual, and toward intuition. That points to a decision that:

- **Weighs consequences first.** The irreversibility of mass-casualty harm deserves heavy weight even at low probability, while the scale of the energy benefit argues against permanent burial.
- **Rests on her own responsibility.** In the existentialist spirit of Sartre and Beauvoir, she cannot hand the decision to "science" or "the process" and be absolved. Even consulting others is her act, and she owns what follows. Beauvoir's emphasis on how our freedom is entangled with others' freedom is a useful reminder that her choice shapes the conditions in which others can act.
- **Honors intuition without being ruled by it.** Her sense of dread about the weapon, and her sense of obligation to the world's poor, are both morally informative. Neither should be silenced. But intuition is vulnerable to self-flattery (the heroic gatekeeper) and to fear, so it needs checking against other minds.

## 3. What to watch for

- **Bad faith (Sartre):** Claiming "I had no choice" in either direction, whether "science must be free" or "it's too dangerous, so the matter is settled."
- **Savior or guardian complacency:** Overconfidence that she alone can judge the risk, or that secrecy will hold. Secrets leak, and others may be close behind.
- **Underestimating the cost of withholding:** Delay in clean energy has its own body count through climate harm and energy poverty. Inaction is not neutral.
- **Overweighting vivid catastrophe:** Intuition amplifies dramatic risks. Test whether the weapon pathway is as feasible as it feels, ideally with independent technical assessment.
- **Drift:** Temporary withholding becoming permanent by default, without any review point.

## 4. A concrete next step in the next 24 hours

Write a one-page private memo with two parts: (a) your honest best estimate of how easy the weapon adaptation really is and how close others are to the discovery, and (b) the names of two or three people with relevant expertise, such as a dual-use research or biosecurity-style review specialist, a senior colleague in your field, and possibly a legal or institutional ethics advisor, whom you could approach in confidence. Then contact one of them to request a confidential conversation, sharing only what is necessary. Do not publish or destroy anything until that conversation has happened.
